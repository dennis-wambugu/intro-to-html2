THE OWL ANATOMY AND HISTORY

Owls are fascinating birds of prey belonging to the order Strigiformes, with over 250 species found across almost every continent except Antarctica

Anatomy & Physical Adaptations

• Tube-Shaped Eyes: Instead of round eyeballs, owls have rigid, cylindrical eye-tubes locked in their sockets, giving them exceptional night vision.
• 270-Degree Head Rotation: Because their eyes cannot roll, owls have 14 neck vertebrae (twice as many as humans) that allow them to turn their heads up to 270 degrees without cutting off blood flow to the brain.
• Asymmetrical Ears: Their ear openings are placed at different heights and angles on their head, creating 3D sound-mapping that lets them hunt prey completely by sound under snow or leaves.
• Silent Flight: Specialized fringe edges on their flight feathers break up air turbulence, allowing them to swoop down on prey completely unheard.
• Zygodactyl Feet: They have two toes pointing forward and two pointing backward, and they can pivot one back toe forward for a stronger grip
